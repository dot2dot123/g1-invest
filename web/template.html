How well does it work? Backtests, method and caveats
Method, data and limitations

Universe & survivorship. Backtest uses historical S&P 500 membership at each month-end (fja05680/sp500 dataset, through Aug 2026), not today's list. Delisted names are kept when Yahoo still has their prices; many acquired/bankrupt names are no longer on Yahoo (about half of members are covered in 2005, ~73% in 2015, ~97% in 2025), so some survivorship bias remains. If a stock stops trading mid-horizon, its proceeds are assumed to sit in SPY. Today's scoring universe = current S&P 500 (Wikipedia table at build) plus every ticker in the five books.

No look-ahead. Fundamentals come from SEC EDGAR companyfacts (XBRL). For each period only the first-filed value is used (no restatements), and it becomes usable 2 days after its filing date. Values older than ~18 months are treated as missing. Training for a test year only uses samples whose full forward window ended before that year starts (purged expanding window, yearly retrain).

Model (v2, pre-registered). For names with SEC fundamentals, the score is a fixed equal-weight composite of four pillars, each built from within-sector percentile ranks: quality (gross profit/assets, net income/assets, low accruals), value (FCF/market cap, EBIT/EV, EBIT/EV vs own 5-year median), low dilution (1-year share change) and balance sheet (low debt/assets, interest coverage). Missing inputs read neutral. Nothing is fitted: the weights were written down and committed (docs/PREREGISTRATION.md) before any test. "P(beat SPY)" and the excess band come from the out-of-sample beat rate of the same score decile. The test universe was the top 1,000 US-listed stocks by market cap at each month-end (price ≥ $5), point-in-time. That universe has survivorship bias: names that were never in the S&P 500 and have since been delisted or acquired are missing, because only today's SEC ticker list is free. Most of the measured edge comes from 2020–2022, and the 36–60m results rest on 1–3 independent periods. The v1 logistic model and a learned ridge model were tested too; neither did better (see the tables above). Price-only names still use the v1 price model. Sentiment: no free point-in-time source.

Overlap. Monthly rebalance dates with 6–60 month windows overlap heavily; "independent periods" ≈ test months ÷ horizon. 48m and 60m have about one independent period, and their training windows start in 2010 (when XBRL begins), so they are not meaningful. t-statistics are scaled for overlap.

Danelfin agreement, Buffett & Munger and Magic 15 boxes. Agree means G1's score at that horizon is in the top 20% (or bottom 20%) and Danelfin's AI Score points the same way (≥7 bullish, ≤4 bearish). Disagree means they point opposite ways; anything else is neutral. Danelfin scores are the Nov 8 portfolio snapshot, so names outside the books show "—". The Buffett & Munger (0–10) and Magic 15 (0–10) boxes are transparent, rule-based proxies of these investors' published principles. They are not the investors' actual views, picks or endorsements. Hover a box for its breakdown. Buffett & Munger is scored on durable ROE, low leverage, stable margins, consistent profits, a gross-margin moat, no dilution, and owner-earnings yield vs the 10-year Treasury. Magic 15 (lean version, live since Nov 11, 2026) is the average of two pillars. Quality compounders (Ackman, Combs, Weschler): ROIC, gross profit/assets, margin stability and low dilution. Downside protection (Marks, Karsh): low debt/assets, interest coverage, shallow 3-year drawdown and low volatility. The earlier six-pillar version also had deep value (Klarman, Pabrai), durable growth (Thiel, Botha, Lin, Fenton, Cohler), macro trend (Druckenmiller) and yield/credit value (Black, Rowan). Those were dropped because they cancelled each other out or added little. The lean version passed its own rule, written down before testing (docs/PREREGISTRATION_LEAN_MAGIC15.md), and replaced the old one. R&D intensity and "simple business" are not scored. All score definitions were committed before testing (docs/PREREGISTRATION_STYLE_SCORES.md). The tests carry the same survivorship-bias caveat as G1.

Refresh. The Refresh button pulls 5 years of weekly prices per name from Yahoo directly in your browser (AllOrigins proxy if Yahoo blocks it), recomputes momentum/volatility/drawdown/beta and price-based valuation (EBIT/EV, FCF yield, sales/EV, book/price, valuation vs history and sector) with the bundled fundamentals, and re-scores with the embedded model. Fundamentals and the models themselves only change when Gary asks for a rebuild in chat. Nothing runs on a schedule.
